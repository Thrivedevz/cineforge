# Horror Directing Briefs

Copy-ready scene briefs for narrated horror. Built for slow-dread pacing: long takes, practical light, reveals without cuts, anomalies over explicit monsters. Every brief follows the 8-block formula. All are model-agnostic.

## Effects Consistency Rules (apply to every brief below)

- Every light source is practical and locked: its position, color temperature, and flicker pattern never change mid-scene. If a hallway bulb flickers, the flicker rhythm is identical in every shot of that location.
- Any entity, creature, or figure gets ONE locked design at first appearance (silhouette, proportions, surface detail, eye treatment). Reference the lock; never let the generator redesign it per scene.
- Darkness must stay readable. Crush the blacks, but the key shape (the door, the figure, the mirror) is always legible. If the frame goes fully black, the shot has failed.
- No freeze-frame scares and no hidden cuts posing as scares. Every reveal is continuous motion: the thing is there because the camera or the action put it there, not because the edit did.
- Camera moves slow. Dread pacing means push-ins, lateral drifts, and locked frames; fast moves are reserved for chase beats only.
- Every scene is real motion: dust in the light beam, curtains breathing, rain on glass. If the subject is still, the environment moves so the frame never dies.

## Brief 1: Hallway Approach, Flickering Bulb

[Output] 8 seconds, 16:9, photorealistic dark horror.

[Subject] No on-screen character. The hallway itself is the subject: narrow residential hallway, peeling wallpaper, one bare bulb on a cord mid-hallway (locked: warm 2700K, irregular flicker with two-second dark gaps).

[World] 2:00 AM interior, power failing. Wallpaper foreground peeling at the seams, the bulb midground on its cord, a closed door at the far end with light seeping under it. Dust hanging in the bulb's cone of light. Silence except the electrical hum.

[Action] Trigger: the bulb dies for a full two seconds. Continuous motion: in the dark gap, the far door opens one inch on its own; when the bulb stutters back on, the door is open wider and a dark shape fills the gap; the camera keeps pushing forward the entire time, never stopping. Visible result: final frame holds on the doorway, the shape now clearly a standing figure, bulb flickering across it.

[Camera] Slow push-in down the hallway's center line, eye level, one continuous move, ending frame: the doorway filling the center third, the figure's silhouette inside it, bulb flare at the top of frame.

[Look] Single warm practical as the only key, deep brown-black shadows, wallpaper texture sharp in the light cone. Flicker is irregular, never strobing. Dust motes sharp inside the beam. No moonlight, no fill.

[Sound] Ambience: electrical hum, house settling ticks. Action sound: bulb buzz dropping out in the dark gap, door hinge creak, a soft footstep with no visible source. Music: none, low sub drone under the push-in. Dialogue: none.

[Constraints] Must remain: bulb design and flicker behavior, hallway layout, 16:9. Likely failure modes: the flicker becomes a regular strobe; the door opens via a hidden cut instead of continuous motion; the figure pops in rather than being revealed by the returning light; wallpaper pattern shifts between flickers. Reject any take where the figure appears without the light revealing it.

Last verified: 2026-10. Model-agnostic.

## Brief 2: The Reveal, Basement Stairs

[Output] 7 seconds, 16:9, photorealistic dark horror.

[Subject] THE WATCHER as defined in the character lock (tall thin silhouette, head tilted 15 degrees left, no visible face, long coat, locked design). Plus a handheld flashlight beam (locked: cool white, tight cone, slight shake).

[World] Night, unfinished basement, concrete stairs descending. Exposed joists foreground, the stairs midground, black doorway at the bottom. Faint mist at the bottom step.

[Action] Trigger: the flashlight beam sweeps down the stairs. Continuous motion: the beam finds the Watcher already standing at the bottom, motionless; the holder's hand shakes harder; the Watcher takes one slow step up toward the camera, coat hanging still as if there is no air. Visible result: the Watcher is now two steps higher and closer, head tilt unchanged, and the flashlight dies.

[Camera] First-person handheld, slight vertical shake, one slow tilt down following the beam, ending frame: black, with the Watcher's silhouette burned as an afterimage in the last lit frame.

[Look] Flashlight as the only source, hard-edged cone, concrete rendered in cold greys, the Watcher rimmed faintly by the beam edge. Mist catches the beam. When the light dies, true black, no fade.

[Sound] Ambience: furnace rumble far off, water drip. Action sound: footsteps on concrete that stop one step too early, flashlight click-off. Music: none. Dialogue: none, breathing only, and the breathing is not the camera holder's.

[Constraints] Must remain: Watcher locked design (tilt, coat, no face), flashlight beam behavior, 16:9. Likely failure modes: the Watcher's proportions shift between the reveal and the step; a face renders by mistake; the stairs change count; the light dies with a fade instead of a hard cut. Reject any take where the Watcher has a visible face.

Last verified: 2026-10. Model-agnostic.

## Brief 3: Chase, Dark House

[Output] 8 seconds, 16:9, photorealistic dark horror.

[Subject] RUNNER as defined in the character lock (barefoot, torn nightshirt, locked design). PURSUER: never fully seen, only a fast-moving shadow mass with too many joints (locked: stays out of direct light, always partial).

[World] Night, suburban house interior, all lights dead. Toppled furniture foreground, hallway midground, moonlight through broken blinds striping the walls. Glass crunching underfoot.

[Action] Trigger: a door slams somewhere below. Continuous motion: the Runner sprints the hallway and vaults the toppled chair without breaking stride; the Pursuer's shadow mass flows along the wall behind, gaining; the Runner throws herself through the bedroom door and slams it. Visible result: the door shudders once from the far side, then silence, and a shadow pools under the door gap.

[Camera] Handheld chase, shoulder height, one continuous run behind the Runner, whip-pan on the vault, ending frame: the closed bedroom door, the shadow pooling beneath it, moonlight stripes across it.

[Look] Moonlight stripes as the only key, cold blue with warm dark beyond, motion blur on the sprint, glass glints on the floor. The Pursuer is always motion-blurred and partial, never a clean render.

[Sound] Ambience: house groan, wind at the broken window. Action sound: bare feet on wood, glass crunch, door slam, the far-side thud, then unnatural silence. Music: pulse matching footfalls, cut dead at the door slam. Dialogue: none.

[Constraints] Must remain: Runner's barefoot nightshirt design, Pursuer stays partial and blurred, 16:9. Likely failure modes: the Pursuer resolves into a clean monster render; the Runner's stride becomes a float with no footfalls; furniture resets between the vault and the landing; the shadow under the door appears before the thud. Reject any take where the Pursuer is fully visible.

Last verified: 2026-10. Model-agnostic.

## Brief 4: Mirror Wrongness, Bathroom

[Output] 7 seconds, 16:9, photorealistic dark horror.

[Subject] MARA as defined in the character lock (mid-30s woman, wet hair, grey robe, locked design). MIRROR MARA: identical except the reflection smiles and Mara does not (locked: the smile is the only difference).

[World] 3:00 AM bathroom, single vanity bulb (locked: warm, steady, no flicker). Tile foreground, mirror midground filling the frame, Mara's back to camera. Steam on the mirror's edges.

[Action] Trigger: Mara leans toward the mirror to wipe the steam. Continuous motion: her hand clears an arc of glass; the reflection clears at a different speed, lagging half a second; she freezes; the reflection keeps moving, leans closer, and smiles. Visible result: final frame holds on the smiling reflection while the real Mara stands motionless, hand still raised.

[Camera] Locked tripod over Mara's shoulder, no movement at all, ending frame: the mirror filling the frame, the smile centered.

[Look] Warm vanity key, soft steam diffusion at the frame edges, tile in desaturated greens. The mirror image slightly sharper than reality. The smile must read clearly but never theatrically.

[Sound] Ambience: faucet drip, extractor fan hum. Action sound: hand squeak on glass, the fan hum dropping half a tone when the reflection moves alone. Music: none. Dialogue: none.

[Constraints] Must remain: Mara's locked design, the smile as the ONLY reflection difference, locked camera, 16:9. Likely failure modes: the reflection drifts into a different person; both Maras smile; the camera drifts; the steam clears symmetrically. Reject any take where the reflection differs from Mara in more than the smile.

Last verified: 2026-10. Model-agnostic.

## Brief 5: Empty Room, Wrong Detail

[Output] 9 seconds, 16:9, photorealistic dark horror.

[Subject] No on-screen character. The room is the subject: a child's bedroom, night-light glow (locked: small rocket-shaped lamp, warm amber, steady), everything in its place except one thing.

[World] 4:00 AM, child's bedroom. Toy blocks foreground, the night-light midground on the dresser, the closet door background, ajar two inches. A crib mobile turning slowly though there is no draft.

[Action] Trigger: the mobile stops turning. Continuous motion: the camera's slow drift continues past the dresser; the closet door opens one more inch on its own; a small shadow detaches from the closet interior and crosses the wall, though nothing in the room could cast it. Visible result: the shadow reaches the crib and stops, and the night-light gutters once without going out.

[Camera] Slow lateral drift at crib height, one continuous move left to right, ending frame: the crib, the shadow stopped at its rail, the night-light flaring back.

[Look] Amber night-light as the only key, deep warm shadows, toys rendered soft at the frame edges. The shadow is darker than the room's natural shadows. No blue moonlight, no fill.

[Sound] Ambience: mobile music-box motif slowing down, house silence. Action sound: the motif stopping mid-note, closet hinge whisper, the night-light's electrical gutter. Music: the dying music-box IS the music. Dialogue: none.

[Constraints] Must remain: night-light design and behavior, room layout, the shadow as the only moving anomaly, 16:9. Likely failure modes: a figure steps out of the closet (too much); the shadow has a visible source; the mobile keeps turning; the night-light goes fully out. Reject any take where the anomaly becomes explicit.

Last verified: 2026-10. Model-agnostic.
